CivicConnect Risk Register - V1.0

Reproduced from PED v3.0 section 5.2 (RSK-01 to RSK-15), which mirrors this controlled register. Wording, ratings and status are as in the PED; nothing has been re-rated or closed here.

Scope note

final.pdf was not available when this was written, so its requirements are not reflected. Check the columns and rating scale against it.

Rating scale

  • Probability and impact: Low, Medium or High, as rated in the PED.
  • Priority: the combined rating from the PED (High, Medium or Low). The PED does not publish the matrix behind it.
  • Status: all fifteen risks are Open in the PED.

Register

ID Description Cause Probability Impact Priority Mitigation Contingency Status
RSK-01 Platform limitations may restrict deployment and cause unexpected downtime. Unanticipated operational costs or exceeding free-tier resource limitations. Medium High High Research and document resource limits for Docker containerisation deployments early. Migrate the chosen backend to an alternative low-cost hosting provider. Open
RSK-02 Frontend, backend and database components may fail to integrate correctly. Components are being developed separately and interfaces may change during implementation. Medium High High Define interfaces and data contracts, integrate components step-by-step, and test integration along the way. Isolate or simplify affected integration points, roll back to the last known working version and focus on critical functionality within the system. Open
RSK-03 Users may gain access to functions outside their authorised role. Incorrect or incomplete role-based authentication and authorisation rules. Medium High High Test Requester, Staff, and Management permissions, secure routes and actions, and test role checks in integration testing. Disable affected functionality until the access control problems have been fixed and tested. Open
RSK-04 Database changes may break existing functionality or create inconsistent data. Schema and data-access changes during ongoing implementation. Medium High High Manage database modifications, document schema changes, and test migrations and queries using development data. Roll back to the old database structure or restore the backups to fix the failed migration or SQL. Open
RSK-05 The project may fall behind schedule due to limited team capacity. The system is being developed by a three-person team alongside academic deadlines. High High High Focus on core requirements, divide tasks, monitor progress, and assess workload regularly. Defer lower-priority or non-core features where permitted and focus effort on required deliverables. Open
RSK-06 Architecture or technology changes may cause rework. Design decisions are still being refined while development is underway. Medium Medium Medium Document design decisions, evaluate proposed architectural modifications before implementing, and ensure modules remain modular. Put off less important or noncritical functionality where it is possible and concentrate on the needed deliverables. Open
RSK-07 Insufficient testing may allow defects into the final system. Time pressure may reduce functional, integration and security testing coverage. Medium High High Associate critical requirements with tests, and perform functional, integration, and security testing iteratively. Roll back to the last approved design or change only the affected pieces of the software. Open
RSK-08 Though there is a backup available, it has never been retrieved, and hence failure translates to data loss. Free-tier hosts do not guarantee automated backups (Supabase free tier requires self-export; Neon free tier keeps a 6-hour history window); no restore procedure is documented. High High High Independent nightly pg_dump plus pg_dumpall --roles-only to off-site encrypted storage; restore drill once per milestone with a committed record; RPO/RTO targets as NFR-02/NFR-03. Rebuild from migrations plus the most recent export, accept the data gap, notify affected data subjects if POPIA s22 is engaged Open
RSK-09 A mass-reporting event (e.g. a network outage) could produce duplicate incidents or duplicate technician notifications under concurrent load. High-concurrency writes to the same clustering key without a serialising constraint. Low Medium Medium The clustering invariant is enforced as a partial unique index plus INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE, not as application-level check-then-insert (section 6.3.1, section 6.3.5). Accept the resulting brief lock contention on the affected row — modelled at roughly two seconds to clear 200 reports at 100 transactions/second — as intended behaviour, not a fault (section 6.3.6). Open
RSK-10 Personal information is retained indefinitely because no retention period was ever agreed with the client, breaching POPIA s14(1). The 7-year figure in the research note is team judgement; the client is the responsible party and has not been asked. Medium High High Confirm retention periods with the client before writing any retention job (OQ-12); implement the sweeps whose periods are defensible now. Freeze deletion, document the position, and disclose the gap to the client as responsible party. Open
RSK-11 Free-text request descriptions and incident notes contain third parties' personal information that survives de-identification of the named user. POPIA s1(g)/(h): free text names people the schema cannot classify. High Medium High Classify these columns as personal information unconditionally; exclude them from the reporting role; redact them in audit payloads; UI guidance not to name colleagues. Manual review and redaction on each s24 request; record the manual step in the procedure. Open
RSK-12 The developer writes an incident report without the version predicate and thereby introduces lost updates again. Optimistic concurrency is a discipline, not a mechanism. Medium High High A single repository update method is the only place the predicate is written; integration test IT-CONC-02; code-review checklist item under PED §6.3. Switch the token to xmin, which the server maintains and nobody can forget — at the cost of exposing an internal. Open
RSK-13 The increase in audit logs uses up the free storage allocation before the creation of any retention task. Audit log is roughly 70 % of projected bytes; the Neon free plan allows 0.5 GB per project. Medium Medium Medium Implement the 3-year audit sweep and the 12-month notification sweep at M3, not M4; monitor table size each milestone. Partition by month and drop partitions, or move to a paid tier, which is RSK-01's contingency. Open
RSK-14 The SQL for migration has not yet run, so a syntax or ordering issue is identified during a demo and not through CI. As there was no execution of the migration SQL before, any kind of syntax error or error in the order will be detected only during the demo phase. High Low Medium The PR that merges the migrations must show a green CI job applying V001–V004 and U004–U001 against a clean PostgreSQL 16 container; blocking, not advisory. Fix forward in a follow-up migration; never edit an applied migration in place. Open
RSK-15 An administrator who is not part of any contract is used as a POPIA operator, violating s21(1). Provider selection has been postponed (RSK-01), and operator obligation has not been mentioned to the customer. Medium High High Verify the operator agreement and the at-rest encryption of the provider and the sub-processor strategy from the provider documentation prior to selection (OQ-09). Use Docker to self-host with team backups, eliminating the operator association completely Open
